# Academic Literature on Intraday / Short-Horizon FX Return & Direction Predictability

*Research vector for binary-algo. Goal context: predict EURUSD direction at a 15-minute horizon, OOS, targeting >75% hit rate. We have already exhausted causal multi-TF TA, cross-pair lead-lag, 10s tick-rule OFI proxy, GBM/NN ensembles (~0.52-0.527 AUC), stat-arb basket residual, daily/weekly context, exogenous peer features, calendar event-timing proxy, and selective prediction (best 15m ~0.632 OOS at 0.2% coverage). True raw-tick OBI is real but decays from 55.3% next-tick to coin-flip by 1 min, gone by 5 min; clean >75% only found at the 3-second horizon.*

---

## TL;DR (most actionable findings for our 15m FX goal)

- **The peer-reviewed consensus is that 15-minute FX direction is essentially a random walk.** A 2026 *International Journal of Forecasting* study using actual FX **limit-order-book** data and modern ML (LASSO, random forest, supervised PCA) across the 1-minute-to-1-hour band finds "**generally low predictability**" across all currency pairs and "supports the EMH" (Petrova, Vilhelmsson & Nordén 2026). This independently corroborates your own ~0.527 AUC ceiling. **A blanket, always-on 75% at 15m is not supported by any credible source.** Treat any such online claim as overfit/leaked until proven OOS.
- **The "79% directional accuracy" you may have seen is EQUITIES at a 5-SECOND horizon with an order-flow peek-ahead** (Aït-Sahalia, Fan, Xue & Zhou, NBER w30366, 2022). At 5s, OOS R²≈10.5% and next-trade direction ≈64% from public TQ data, rising to 79% only with a (cheating) signal on incoming order-flow direction. **It vanishes to coin-flip by ~minutes** — exactly your decay finding. This is *not* a 15m signal; do not chase it.
- **The single most robust, orthogonal, reproducible intraday FX edge in the literature is the fixing-window reversal.** Krohn, Mueller & Whelan (*Journal of Finance*, 2024) document a **W-shaped, around-the-clock return pattern**: USD systematically appreciates into the Tokyo / ECB(2:15pm CET) / London(4pm) fixes and reverses after, statistically significant across all G10 over 21 years. Magnitudes are small (single-digit bps), so it is a **conditioning/timing overlay**, not a standalone 75% engine — but it is a real, mechanistic, non-TA signal you have not explicitly exploited.
- **Intraday momentum (Gao-Han-Li-Zhou) is real but weak in FX.** First-part-of-day return predicts last-30-min return; in *currency futures* the timing strategy yields Sharpe ≈ **0.43-0.49** (Elsasser/Baltussen-Da-Lammers-Martens), predictive R² ~1-2%. That is roughly **53-55% directional**, not 75%, and it lives at the **daily-close anchor**, which FX (24h, no single close) only weakly has. Useful as a feature, not a solution.
- **Predictability in FX is concentrated, not pervasive — it clusters around scheduled macro news and around the fixes.** Andersen-Bollerslev-Diebold-Vega show macro announcements drive real-time price discovery; ~1/4 of FX jumps map to a specific announcement. Outside those windows EURUSD behaves like a random walk. **Strategy implication: don't try to predict every 15m bar; predict only the conditional windows where an edge mechanistically exists (news + fix).**
- **The genuinely informative signal in FX is *disaggregated customer order flow*, which you do NOT have.** Menkhoff-Sarno-Schmeling-Schrimpf and Rime-Sarno-Sojli show *who* is trading (corporate vs. financial vs. real-money vs. dealer) is highly informative about future rates — far more than the anonymous tick-rule OFI proxy you built. Your tick-rule sign is the weakest version of this. This is largely a **data-access gap**, not a modeling gap.
- **Taylor-rule / interest-differential fundamentals give OOS edge only at the 1-month+ horizon** (Molodtsova-Papell), irrelevant at 15m except as a slow regime/drift conditioner.
- **Net recommendation:** stop hunting an always-on 15m direction signal (the literature says it isn't there). Re-frame as **conditional/selective prediction in mechanistically-special windows** (fix reversals, post-news drift/reversal, options-gamma regimes) and accept that the realistic OOS prize is high accuracy at *low coverage*, which is exactly where your 0.632@0.2% result already points.

## Key findings (detailed, with inline citations)

### 1. The efficient-markets baseline is strong at 1min-1hour in FX — confirmed with real LOB data
Petrova, Vilhelmsson & Nordén, *"Assessing cross-currency predictability in forex markets: Insights from limit order book data,"* **International Journal of Forecasting (2026)** (SSRN 5523878; ScienceDirect S0169207025001281). They use **detailed FX limit-order-book data**, multiple pairs, horizons **1 minute to 1 hour**, factor-augmented regressions, supervised/unsupervised PCA, LASSO, random forest. Result: "**generally low predictability across various models, supporting the Efficient Market Hypothesis.**" This is the most direct, modern, methodologically-serious refutation of easy 15m FX predictability — and it had *better* data (full LOB) than your 10s bars + tick-rule proxy, yet still found little. **This is your strongest reality check: the ceiling you hit is the literature's ceiling.**

### 2. High-frequency predictability is large but only at seconds, and it is equities, not FX
Aït-Sahalia, Fan, Xue & Zhou, *"How and When are High-Frequency Stock Returns Predictable?"* **NBER WP 30366 (2022).** Verbatim from the paper: a "**10.5% out-of-sample R² for predicting 5-second returns**… and an accuracy of **64% for predicting the direction of the next trade**" from past trade-and-quote data; the dominant predictor is "**the imbalance in the limit order book.**" Simulating an imperfect peek at incoming order flow lifts "**5-second return R² from 14.0% up to 27.1% and the price direction accuracy from 68.3% up to 79.0%.**" Crucially they state predictability "**vanishes to 0**" as the horizon lengthens. **Direct read-across to you: the 79% is (a) equities (SPY-universe stocks), (b) 5-second, (c) partly an information-advantage simulation. It validates your 3-second clean-edge / fast-decay finding and confirms 15m needs a different source.**

### 3. The most robust orthogonal intraday FX pattern: fixing-window reversals
Krohn, Mueller & Whelan, *"Foreign Exchange Fixings and Returns Around the Clock,"* **Journal of Finance 79 (2024)** (also BIS workshop slides, Bank of Canada SWP). Findings: the USD **appreciates in the run-up to the Tokyo (9:55 JST), ECB (2:15pm CET) and London (4:00pm) fixes and depreciates after**, producing a **W-shaped cumulative-return pattern around the 24h clock**. "Pervasive and highly statistically significant" for the top nine currencies over 21 years; "robust over time, present in all G10, not driven by calendar effects." The reversal **nets to zero on average** and pre-fix drift is partly **absorbed by the effective bid-ask spread** — i.e. magnitudes are small (a few bps), so it is exploitable as *conditioning/direction-tilt within those specific 15-30 min windows*, not as a free lunch. Related: NBER w23327 ("Did the Reform Fix the London Fix Problem?") and arXiv 1501.07778 (4pm-fix microstructure) document the same window with order-flow detail (futures flows positive 3:45→4:00, then reverse for ~30 min). **This is a concrete, mechanistic, non-TA, time-conditioned directional signal you have not isolated.**

### 4. Intraday momentum (first-part predicts last-part) — real, but weak and equity-centric
Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, *"Market Intraday Momentum"* (SSRN 2440866; J. Financial Economics 2018) and the earlier *"Intraday Momentum: The First Half-Hour Return Predicts the Last Half-Hour Return"* (SSRN 2552752). On SPY 1993-2013: first-half-hour return predicts last-half-hour return, **predictive R² 1.6% (2.6% combined with the 12th half-hour), OOS R² ~1.4%, timing-strategy Sharpe ~1.08**, *stronger on high-vol, high-volume, recession, and macro-news-release days*. The mechanism (Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, *"Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum,"* **JFE 2021**, SSRN 3760365) is **gamma-hedging by option/leveraged-ETF market-makers who are net short gamma** → they buy as price rises, sell as it falls → trend into the close. Global-futures evidence (Lim/Cheng-style "Intraday time series momentum: global evidence," J. Financial Markets 2022, centaur.reading 95566) extends to 16 markets; **for currency futures the effect is the weakest, Sharpe ≈ 0.43-0.49.** **Read-across: anchored to a daily close/open structure FX largely lacks; the strongest analog for FX is the fix windows (§3) and the macro-news windows (§5). Worth a feature, not a strategy.**

### 5. FX predictability is concentrated around scheduled macro news
Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega, *"Micro Effects of Macro Announcements: Real-Time Price Discovery in Foreign Exchange"* (AER 2003) — announcements drive sharp, fast, directional real-time adjustment. Lahaye-Laurent-Neely and related work: roughly **one-quarter of intraday FX jumps map to a specific US macro announcement**; news-related jumps carry the predictability, clustering and cojumps, whereas non-news periods look like a random walk. **Read-across: your "calendar event-timing proxy was redundant with time-of-day" result is because you proxied the *volatility-seasonality*, not the *directional surprise*. The directional content is in the *sign and size of the surprise vs. consensus* and the *first-few-seconds reaction*, which a vol-window proxy cannot capture. This is a different, under-exploited lever.**

### 6. The truly informative FX signal is disaggregated customer order flow (a data gap, not a model gap)
Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling & Schrimpf, *"Information Flows in FX Markets: Dissecting Customer Currency Trades"* (BIS WP 405; J. Finance 2016): order flow **disaggregated by customer type** (corporate, real-money/asset-manager, leveraged/hedge-fund, private) is "highly informative about future exchange rates," and groups differ markedly in predictive ability. Rime, Sarno & Sojli (JIE 2010): **dealer order flow anticipates returns better than any single client group.** Evans & Lyons (JPE 2002, NBER 7317) established the foundational contemporaneous order-flow→rate link (daily R²>50%), **but** Berger et al. (Fed IFDP 830, 2005) found the EBS relationship is **contemporaneous, with no out-of-sample *predictive* power of past order flow for future rates.** **Read-across: your anonymous tick-rule OFI is the weakest, least-informative version. The lift in the literature comes from *counterparty identity*, which is private dealer/PB data you do not have. This explains why your OFI added ~0 lift and is unlikely to improve without identified flow.**

### 7. Fundamentals only matter at long horizons
Molodtsova & Papell, *"Out-of-Sample Exchange Rate Predictability with Taylor Rule Fundamentals"* (JIE 2009): significant OOS predictability for 11/12 USD pairs **at the 1-month horizon**. Meese-Rogoff (1983) disconnect dominates at short horizons. **Read-across: irrelevant to 15m except as a slow regime/drift conditioner (interest-rate-differential carry tilt), already partially in your daily context.**

### 8. Cross-asset / cross-currency lead-lag exists at HF but is fast and thin
arXiv 1906.10388 ("Lead-Lag Relationships in FX Markets") and HF lead-lag work (Huth-Abergel 1111.7103) reach ~**60% next-midquote-move accuracy of a lagger from a leader at the 1-minute scale**, with intraday seasonality concentrated at macro releases and the US open. Equity→rates→FX transmission is documented but operates in **seconds-to-minutes**, decaying fast (same family as your already-exhausted cross-pair lead-lag). **Read-across: you've largely done this within USD pairs; the un-tried extension is *cross-asset* leaders (S&P futures, UST/Bund futures, front WTI) into EURUSD, conditioned on the news/open windows where the lead-lag actually concentrates.**

## Concrete techniques / features / architectures to try

1. **Fix-window directional overlay (highest-novelty, mechanistic).** Build event-time features anchored to the **Tokyo (00:55 ET / 09:55 JST), ECB (08:15 ET / 14:15 CET) and London (11:00 ET / 16:00 BST) fixes**. For each 15m bar, encode minutes-to-fix and minutes-since-fix, plus the cumulative pre-fix drift. Train a *separate* conditional model that only predicts in the ±30-min fix windows, exploiting the documented pre-fix drift / post-fix reversal sign. Expect *small* edge but in a regime where direction is mechanistically non-random. (Krohn-Mueller-Whelan 2024.)

2. **Surprise-conditioned post-news drift, not vol-seasonality.** Replace your "release-window vol proxy" with the **signed standardized surprise** = (actual − consensus)/historical-σ for the top EURUSD-moving releases (NFP, CPI, FOMC, ECB, PMI, GDP). Feature the first 10-60s post-release return as a state, then predict the 15m continuation/fade. Requires a consensus-vs-actual calendar feed (see Data). (Andersen-Bollerslev-Diebold-Vega 2003; Lahaye et al.)

3. **Options-gamma regime conditioner.** Approximate dealer net gamma in EURUSD from listed FX options open interest / CME FX options, or use 25-delta risk-reversal and butterfly skew as a proxy for positioning. Hypothesis (from Baltussen-Da 2021 mechanism): in **short-gamma (negative GEX) regimes intraday moves trend; in long-gamma they mean-revert**. Use the regime to *switch the sign of your momentum/reversal feature*, rather than as a static feature. This is the closest FX analog of the equity intraday-momentum mechanism.

4. **Cross-asset leaders into EURUSD, windowed.** Add 15m/5m signed returns of **ES (S&P futures), front WTI, and the UST 2y/10y or Bund futures** as leaders, but *interact them with the news/US-open dummy* where lead-lag concentrates. Plain unconditioned cross-asset features will wash out (you saw this with cross-pair); the conditioning is the new idea. (arXiv 1906.10388; cross-asset lead-lag literature.)

5. **Selective prediction tied to *mechanistic* state, not just |p−0.5|.** Your 0.632@0.2% came from abstaining on confidence. Re-derive coverage from the *union of mechanistically-special windows* (fix ±30m, news ±15m, short-gamma regime) and measure hit-rate inside vs. outside. The literature predicts the edge is almost entirely inside; this should raise accuracy-at-coverage and is more robust than a black-box confidence gate.

6. **DeepLOB-style architecture only if you upgrade the target horizon down OR the data up.** DeepLOB (Zhang-Zohren-Roberts, IEEE TSP 2019, arXiv 1808.03668) — CNN+LSTM on raw multi-level LOB — gives stable OOS mid-price *direction* accuracy, but the published wins are equities at **10-100 event horizons (seconds)**, matching §2's decay. Applying it to your raw FX ticks for the **3s-30s horizon** is plausible (you already have a clean 3s edge); applying it to 15m is not supported. Use stationary features (mid-price *differences* and normalized depth) per Tsantekidis et al. (arXiv 1810.09965) to keep it trainable.

7. **Identified-flow proxy via venue/size microstructure.** You can't buy dealer flow, but you can approximate *customer-type* by **trade-size clustering and quote-size dynamics** in your tick data (large "real-money"-like clips vs. small HFT clips), since the literature's predictability comes from counterparty type. Build size-bucketed signed-flow features rather than a single tick-rule OFI. Lower-confidence but the only path to the §6 signal without private data.

## Reported results & CREDIBILITY assessment

**Credible / reproducible (peer-reviewed, OOS-aware, sober magnitudes):**
- Petrova-Vilhelmsson-Nordén 2026 (IJF): *low* 1min-1h FX predictability with real LOB data. **High credibility; it's a null result, hardest to fake.** Directly anchors your expectations.
- Aït-Sahalia et al. 2022 (NBER): explicit OOS R² (10.5% @5s), explicit decay to zero, honest peek-ahead simulation. **High credibility; but equities + 5s, not transferable to 15m FX.**
- Krohn-Mueller-Whelan 2024 (J. Finance): fix reversals. **High credibility, top journal, 21y, all-G10, robustness to calendar effects, and they note the spread eats much of it — honest about economics.**
- Gao-Han-Li-Zhou (JFE 2018) + Baltussen-Da et al. (JFE 2021): intraday momentum + gamma mechanism. **High credibility; FX leg is explicitly weak (Sharpe ~0.43-0.49), which is the honest part.**
- Menkhoff et al. / Rime-Sarno-Sojli: customer-flow informativeness. **High credibility, but relies on proprietary flow you lack.**

**Overfit / hype / leakage risks (treat skeptically):**
- Single-paper "EUR/USD direction with ML" results: arXiv 2409.04471 reports only **58.5% one-day-ahead** (54-55% with proper PCA/feature handling), and notes models can collapse to **AUC≈0.495 (coin-flip)** with poor generalization — itself an honest cautionary tale. The frequent online "70-90% accuracy" forex-ML claims are not reproducible and typically suffer: **look-ahead/leakage** (using the bar's own close, or future-derived labels/normalization), **predicting price level not return-sign** (trivially "accurate" because levels autocorrelate), **survivorship/period selection**, and **no transaction costs/spread**. None survive a strict OOS, costed, sign-of-return protocol. **Your 2024/2025-test, 2026-held-out design already defends against most of these — keep it.**
- Practitioner blogs on "GEX/gamma walls" for intraday direction (SpotGamma/Bookmap/MenthorQ): the *mechanism* is academically grounded (Baltussen-Da 2021), but the specific "magnet level" claims are marketing and largely equity/0DTE-index, not FX. Use the regime idea, distrust the level claims.

**Key leakage traps to actively guard in any of the above features:**
- Fix/news event-time features must use **only information available before the bar's open** for the prediction; the *surprise* is known at release time but the *continuation* must be forecast forward.
- Risk-reversal / gamma proxies are often published with a delay — use lagged, point-in-time snapshots.
- Cross-asset leaders must be strictly **time-aligned with no forward bleakage** across the 15m boundary (mind exchange vs. OTC timestamps and DST).

## Relevance & priority for OUR project

**HIGH**
- **Fix-window conditional direction model (feature 1, §3).** Orthogonal to all 239 TA features, mechanistic, free, OOS-testable immediately. Most likely to add genuine (if small, low-coverage) directional edge. Does *not* overlap your "event-time vol proxy" because that captured volatility seasonality, not the directional pre-fix drift / post-fix reversal.
- **Surprise-conditioned, sign-aware news model (feature 2, §5).** Distinct from your redundant calendar-vol proxy — it uses the *signed surprise* and *immediate reaction*, the actual directional carrier. Needs a consensus feed; medium effort, high orthogonality.
- **Selective prediction redefined by mechanistic windows (feature 5).** Cheap to test, directly extends your existing 0.632@0.2% result, and the literature predicts the edge concentrates exactly there. Likely the single best ROI given what you already have.

**MED**
- **Options-gamma regime sign-switch (feature 3).** Strong mechanism (Baltussen-Da 2021) but FX gamma data is the friction; CME OI proxy is a reasonable free start. Interacts with momentum/reversal features you already have by *gating their sign*.
- **Cross-asset leaders windowed (feature 4).** You've done cross-*pair*; cross-*asset* (ES/rates/oil) conditioned on news/US-open is the un-tried twist. Modest cost via Databento. Risk: washes out unconditioned (as your cross-pair did).
- **Deep-LOB on the 3-30s horizon (feature 6).** Aligns with your one clean win (3s). Not a 15m play, but could *extend* the fast edge slightly and feed a faster sub-model. Reframes the horizon rather than the goal.

**LOW**
- **Taylor-rule / fundamentals (§7).** Only 1-month+ horizon; at best a slow drift conditioner you partly have.
- **Anonymous tick-rule OFI refinements (§6 baseline).** Already ~0 lift; literature says the value is in *identity*, which anonymous flow can't recover. Don't reinvest here except via the size-bucketed proxy (feature 7).
- **Generic forex-ML "high-accuracy" replications.** Hype-dominated; your protocol already exceeds their rigor. Mining these is negative-EV.

**Hard truth from the literature for the 75% target:** No credible, reproducible source shows **always-on** 75% direction at 15m in a liquid FX major — the best modern study with *better data than yours* finds near-EMH. The achievable, defensible prize is **high accuracy at low coverage inside mechanistically-special windows** (fix, news, gamma regime). Your existing selective-prediction result is the right shape; the contribution of this literature is *which windows to point it at* and *which orthogonal states to condition on*.
