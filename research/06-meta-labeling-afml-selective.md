# Meta-Labeling, Triple-Barrier & AFML Methods to Boost Selective Precision

**Research vector 06** — applying Marcos López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*
(AFML) toolkit to our 15-minute EURUSD direction problem, with a hard focus on whether **meta-labeling**
can lift the **hit-rate of our high-confidence subset** toward 75%.

> Framing for this project: we already do crude selective prediction (bet only when `|p−0.5|` is large)
> and top out at ~0.632 OOS accuracy at 0.2% coverage. Meta-labeling is the *principled* version of
> exactly that idea. The central question this report answers is **not** "does meta-labeling make money"
> (many blogs claim it does) but **"can a second model meaningfully raise the conditional accuracy
> P(correct | we bet) above what naive |p−0.5| thresholding already gives us, on a near-efficient
> instrument where the base directional edge is ≈0.51?"** The honest answer is nuanced and below.

---

## TL;DR (most actionable findings for our 15m FX goal)

- **Meta-labeling cannot create directional edge that isn't there.** It is a *precision-raising filter*,
  not a side-predictor. Its job is to learn **when our existing primary model is right vs wrong** and
  bet only then. If our primary's edge is ~0.51 and roughly uniform across the feature space, a meta-model
  has nothing to condition on and will not help. It pays off **only if "primary is correct" is itself
  predictable** — i.e. there exist regimes/feature-regions where accuracy is materially >0.51. That is the
  one empirical question worth testing, and it is **cheap to test** (see High-priority item #1).
- **The headline "accuracy jumps from 20% → 77%" numbers in the popular Hudson & Thames write-up are
  measuring a *different target* (meta-label accuracy: did-the-trade-work) than directional accuracy.**
  They are real but not comparable to our 0.75 directional goal; treat as *credible-but-not-apples-to-apples*.
  Do not import them as evidence that 15m direction is solvable.
- **Triple-barrier labeling is strictly better than our fixed-horizon up/down label** for the *meta*
  problem, because it (a) makes the label economically meaningful (vol-scaled profit-take / stop / timeout),
  (b) injects transaction-cost realism, and (c) gives the meta-model a cleaner "was this trade good"
  target than a raw 15m sign. **Adopt it for the meta-label even if the primary stays sign-of-return.**
- **Our cross-validation is probably the single biggest latent leakage risk.** With a 15m horizon and
  overlapping triple-barrier paths, naive K-fold or even a single chronological split leaks. **Purged
  K-fold + embargo (and ideally Combinatorial Purged CV / CPCV)** is mandatory and gives us a *distribution*
  of OOS accuracies instead of one number — essential for not fooling ourselves at 0.2% coverage where
  n is tiny.
- **Sample uniqueness / sequential bootstrap matters a lot at 15m with overlapping labels.** Overlapping
  triple-barrier events are highly redundant; standard bagging over-counts them and inflates confidence.
  Average-uniqueness weighting + sequential bootstrap de-bias the meta-model and its feature importances.
- **Fractional differentiation is a plausible *new orthogonal feature source* we have not tried.** Returns
  (d=1) throw away all level/memory; fracdiff finds the minimum `d` that is ADF-stationary while keeping
  maximal memory. For FX this surfaces *long-memory in the price level / spread / basket* that pure returns
  destroy — a candidate signal that is by construction not in our 239 return-based TA features.
- **The right success metric is the Deflated Sharpe Ratio / PBO, not raw accuracy.** We have run 17+ variants;
  by DSR/PBO logic our *selection* process is itself overfitting-prone. Reporting OOS accuracy without
  deflating for "number of trials" is how the 0.632 could be a fluke. Compute PBO via CSCV before believing
  any selective number.
- **Conformal prediction is a modern, distribution-free alternative/complement to meta-labeling** for the
  abstain decision, with *finite-sample coverage guarantees* — directly relevant to "bet only on the
  top X%" and worth a small experiment alongside meta-labeling.

## Key findings (detailed, with inline citations)

### 1. What meta-labeling actually is (and what it is not)

Meta-labeling **decouples side (direction) from size (bet/no-bet)**. A **primary model M1** predicts
direction Y ∈ {−1,0,1}; a **secondary model M2** is a *binary* classifier whose target is the **meta-label
F ∈ {0,1} = "was M1's call profitable?"** M2 outputs a probability interpreted as confidence, used to
**filter trades or size positions** ([Meta-Labeling, Wikipedia — "General architecture"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling)).
Crucially: *"The secondary model does not generate additional trading signals; instead, its function is
to filter out weaker signals generated by the primary model. Consequently, the performance of the secondary
model depends heavily on the accuracy of the primary model"* ([Wikipedia, "Notes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling)).
This is the load-bearing caveat for us: **garbage primary → garbage meta**.

The mechanism for *why precision rises*: meta-labeling exploits the **precision/recall tradeoff**. The
primary is tuned for **high recall** (catch all the up-moves, tolerate false positives); the meta-model
then **trades recall for precision** by suppressing the false positives, raising **F1 and Sharpe** without
needing a better side-predictor ([Hudson & Thames, "Does Meta Labeling Add to Signal Efficacy?"](https://hudsonthames.org/does-meta-labeling-add-to-signal-efficacy-triple-barrier-method/);
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling)). This is **exactly our selective-prediction problem
stated correctly**: we want to maximize P(correct | bet) on a small, high-confidence subset.

### 2. The three components / heterogeneous architectures (Joubert/Meyer 2022)

Joubert deconstructs meta-labeling into components and shows via a controlled experiment how each helps:
the **information advantage** ("one of the aims of the secondary model is to better exploit information
that the primary model did not fully utilise") and **modeling for false positives** ([Meyer, Joubert & Alfeus, "Meta-Labeling Architecture," *J. Financial Data Science*, 2022](https://jfds.pm-research.com/content/early/2022/09/16/jfds.2022.1.108);
[Joubert, "Meta-Labeling: Theory and Framework," SSRN 4032018, 2022](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4032018)).
A follow-up studies **ensemble meta-labeling** — whether ensembles better extract regimes / detect false
positives ([Ensemble Meta-Labeling, *JFDS* / UCL Discovery, 2022](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10169066/)).
Practical takeaway: **feed M2 not just the primary's probability but the raw features *and* primary-derived
meta-features** (the primary's confidence, recent primary hit-rate, vol regime, time-of-day) so it can find
the "information advantage" region where M1 is reliable.

### 3. The widely-cited efficacy numbers — and why they overstate things for us

The most-quoted empirical demo: on a Bollinger-Band mean-reversion strategy on S&P500 e-mini, after adding
a Random Forest meta-model on features (14-day RSI, vol, MAs, 1–5 day autocorr/momentum), *"the accuracy
jumps from 20% to 77%. The precision of correct trades also jumps from 0.21 to 0.39"* on validation
([Hudson & Thames efficacy paper](https://hudsonthames.org/does-meta-labeling-add-to-signal-efficacy-triple-barrier-method/)).
**Read this carefully:** "accuracy" here is the **meta-label accuracy** (did the model correctly predict
trade/no-trade), *not* directional accuracy of EURUSD. The 20%→77% jump is largely a **base-rate artifact**:
when most signals are no-trade, predicting "no-trade" scores high accuracy. **Precision 0.21→0.39 is the
honest number, and it is well below 0.75.** So even the flagship example, read correctly, does *not* deliver
a 75% precision oracle.

### 4. Triple-barrier labeling beats our fixed-horizon sign label

The triple-barrier method sets an **upper barrier (profit-take), lower barrier (stop), and vertical barrier
(max holding)**, both horizontal barriers **scaled by local volatility** (rolling σ of returns), and labels
by **which barrier is hit first** ([López de Prado, AFML Ch.3, via O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/advances-in-financial/9781119482086/c03.xhtml);
[QuantInsti, "Triple Barrier Method"](https://blog.quantinsti.com/triple-barrier-method-gpu-python/)).
Why it matters for *us*: our current label is "sign of 15m return," which "tends to learn from many noisy
observations" and forces the model to "frequently switch between long and short … incur a lot of costs,
particularly in intraday trading" ([AXLFI, "Triple Barrier Method"](https://axlfi.com/blog/triple-barrier-method)).
Vol-scaling fixes the regime problem: *"a 2% move … may be meaningful in low volatility but noise in high
volatility"* ([QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/triple-barrier-method-gpu-python/)). **Tuning caveat:**
"If the barriers are too tight, labels become noisy; if too wide, many events expire at the vertical
barrier" ([QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/triple-barrier-method-gpu-python/)).

### 5. Sample uniqueness, concurrency & sequential bootstrap

Triple-barrier labels **overlap in time** → observations are **not IID** and are **concurrent** (share
common returns). Standard ML and standard bagging over-weight redundant, overlapping samples. AFML's fix:
compute **average uniqueness** of each label (uniqueness = 1/(c+1) per overlapped bar, averaged), and draw
training samples via **sequential bootstrap**, which preferentially picks low-overlap (high-uniqueness)
observations ([mlfinlab, "Sequential Bootstrapping"](https://www.mlfinlab.com/en/latest/sampling/sequential_boot.html);
[Hudson & Thames, "Bagging … Sequential Bootstrapping"](https://hudsonthames.org/bagging-in-financial-machine-learning-sequential-bootstrapping-python/)).
With our 15m overlapping windows this is **directly load-bearing** — without it the meta-model's confidence
and feature importances are inflated by redundancy. **Warning from AFML Ch.4:** *return-attribution* sample
weighting (weighting by realized return) can backfire — "the dramatic failure of return attribution
weighting … demonstrates that conflating an observation's informational uniqueness with its financial return
leads to pathological model behavior"; prefer **average-uniqueness weighting** ([MQL5, "Label Concurrency"](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/19850); AFML Ch.4).

### 6. Fractional differentiation — stationarity *without* killing memory

Integer differencing forces a binary choice: raw price (d=0) keeps memory but is non-stationary; returns
(d=1) are stationary but "discard all price-level information." **Fractional differentiation finds the
minimum real-valued `d*`** that passes the ADF stationarity test while **preserving maximal memory**
(correlation to the original series), typically via **Fixed-Width Fractional Differencing (FFD)** with a
constant weight vector ([López de Prado, AFML Ch.5](https://hudsonthames.org/fractional-differentiation/);
[mlfinpy, "Fractionally Differentiated"](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FractionalDifferentiated.html)).
**Why this is a genuinely new feature source for us:** our 239 features and the −0.03 autocorr finding are
all built on **returns**, which are d=1 and memoryless by construction. Fracdiff on the **price level,
the bid-ask spread, the USD-basket level, or the cross-pair spread** can expose *long-memory* that returns
destroy — a candidate orthogonal signal we have **not** tried.

### 7. Purged K-fold, embargo, and Combinatorial Purged CV (CPCV)

Standard K-fold is "fundamentally invalid for financial time series" because labels overlap and markets
have memory ([QuantInsti, "Cross Validation in Finance"](https://blog.quantinsti.com/cross-validation-embargo-purging-combinatorial/)).
**Purging** removes training observations whose label window overlaps the test window; **embargo** drops a
small % of observations immediately after each test block to kill serial-correlation leakage. **CPCV** uses
k−p test folds (p>1), purges+embargoes, and **recombines many train/test paths to yield a *distribution* of
OOS performance** rather than a single point estimate ([skfolio, CombinatorialPurgedCV](https://skfolio.org/generated/skfolio.model_selection.CombinatorialPurgedCV.html);
[QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/cross-validation-embargo-purging-combinatorial/)). For us at 0.2%
coverage, **a single OOS accuracy number is statistically fragile** — CPCV's distribution tells us whether
0.632 is robust or a lucky path.

### 8. Backtest overfitting: PBO and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio

**Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)**, via **Combinatorially-Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV)**,
estimates the probability that the strategy we *selected* (best in-sample of N trials) underperforms the
median out-of-sample ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting,"
*J. Computational Finance*, 2015 — SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)).
The **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)** corrects a Sharpe for **selection bias under multiple testing,
non-normality (skew/kurtosis), and sample length**, using the **variance of Sharpe across trials and the
number of (effectively independent) trials** ([Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio,"
*J. Portfolio Management*, 2014 — SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551);
[Deflated Sharpe ratio, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio)). **Direct relevance:
we have already run 17+ variants.** Our effective N is large; any "best OOS accuracy" must be deflated
before we believe it. This is the discipline that separates our 3-second result (likely real) from a
15-minute mirage.

### 9. Cost-sensitive / focal loss / class imbalance for rare high-edge setups

If we reframe to **"rare, high-edge setups"** (the only places a meta-model could find >0.51), the positive
class is tiny → imbalanced learning. **Focal loss** down-weights easy/abundant examples (`γ` controls it)
and up-weights hard/minority ones (`α`), concentrating the model on the rare high-edge events
([Lin et al. focal loss; review in MDPI Symmetry 13(1):4](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/13/1/4)).
**Cost-sensitive learning** multiplies the loss by a class/instance weight to penalize missing the rare
class ([Springer, "Cost-sensitive learning for imbalanced data," 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-023-10652-8)).
**Caveat:** these reshape the decision threshold/recall; they do **not** add information. They are useful
for the meta-model's *bet/no-bet* calibration, not for conjuring directional edge.

### 10. Conformal prediction — a guaranteed-coverage alternative for the abstain decision

Conformal prediction yields **prediction sets with finite-sample coverage guarantees** (e.g. true label
in set ≥90% of the time) and underpins **selective classification with abstention**: *"if a model generates
a buy signal but the conformal forecast indicates a high probability of error, a risk manager may … abstain
from the trade"* ([AlgoTrading101, "Conformal Prediction Guide"](https://algotrading101.com/learn/conformal-prediction-guide/);
[arXiv 2512.12844, "Selective Conformal Risk Control"](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12844)). This is a
**distribution-free competitor to meta-labeling for the *when-to-bet* decision** and trades coverage for
precision with explicit guarantees — attractive given our tiny-n selective regime.

## Concrete techniques / features / architectures to try (implementable)

1. **Meta-labeling layer on top of our existing best primary (V16/V17).**
   - Keep the current LGBM/ensemble as **M1** (side). Generate its prediction + probability on train.
   - Build **triple-barrier meta-labels** F∈{0,1}: for each M1 signal, place vol-scaled PT/SL barriers
     (e.g. ±1.0–1.5× rolling σ of 15m returns) and a vertical barrier at the 15m horizon (or longer, e.g.
     30–60m, to give the directional call room). F=1 iff the trade hits PT before SL/timeout **net of a
     realistic spread cost** (EURUSD ~0.1–0.2 pip; use our tick bid/ask to be exact).
   - **M2** = gradient-boosted classifier on: M1 probability, `|p−0.5|`, raw top features, recent M1
     rolling hit-rate, realized-vol regime, time-of-day/session, spread width, USD-basket dispersion.
     Threshold M2's probability to define "bet." **Compare P(correct | bet) vs our naive `|p−0.5|`
     baseline at matched coverage.** This is the core experiment.

2. **Cheap pre-test before building anything (do this first, ~1 hour):** bucket existing OOS predictions by
   `|p−0.5|` decile **and** by vol regime / session / day-of-week, and measure conditional directional
   accuracy per bucket. **If no bucket materially exceeds 0.51–0.55, meta-labeling will not reach 0.75** —
   this falsifies the whole vector quickly and honestly.

3. **Fractional-differentiation feature set (new orthogonal source).** Compute `d*` (binary-search ADF at
   5%) on: EURUSD mid **level**, log-spread, USD-basket index level, and EURUSD−(basket) residual level.
   Feed the FFD series + their short lags as features to both M1 and M2. This is the most likely place to
   find signal our return-only features missed.

4. **Sample-weighting + sequential bootstrap** for M2: compute average uniqueness of the overlapping
   triple-barrier events; train M2 with uniqueness sample weights and/or a bagging classifier using
   sequential bootstrap. **Avoid return-attribution weighting** (documented to misbehave).

5. **Replace eval harness with Purged K-fold + embargo, then CPCV.** Purge train labels overlapping each
   test fold; embargo ~1× the label span (e.g. drop ~1–2 h after each test block). Report the **CPCV
   distribution** of selective accuracy, not a point. Embargo % per AFML ≈ small single-digit % of samples.

6. **Deflate everything.** After model selection, compute **PBO via CSCV** over our variant grid and a
   **DSR** for the selective strategy (feed number of trials = our true count of tried variants, plus
   skew/kurtosis of the bet returns). Only trust a result with low PBO and DSR-positive at chosen confidence.

7. **Focal-loss / cost-sensitive M2** if meta-labels are very imbalanced (rare profitable setups): start
   `γ≈2`, tune `α` to the positive base rate; or class-weight = inverse frequency.

8. **Conformal selective head (parallel experiment):** wrap M1 in split/Mondrian conformal prediction;
   abstain unless the singleton prediction set holds at target coverage. Compare its precision-vs-coverage
   curve against the meta-model's. Mondrian (conditional on vol regime) is the right variant for FX.

**Cross-cutting leakage/illusion risks specific to us:**
- **The selective-prediction trap:** any method that reports accuracy on a tiny high-confidence subset is
  vulnerable to selection-bias illusions; at 0.2% coverage our OOS n is small enough that 0.63 vs 0.50 may
  not be significant. **CPCV distribution + DSR are mandatory** before claiming improvement.
- **Meta-label leakage via barrier overlap:** if triple-barrier windows extend past fold boundaries, the
  meta-model sees the future. Purge + embargo on the **barrier exit time**, not the entry time.
- **Cost realism:** meta-labeling's apparent edge often comes from "trade less" → ensure the comparison
  holds *coverage* constant, else you're just measuring fewer trades, not better ones.

## Relevance & priority for OUR project

How this interacts with what we've already ruled out:
- We **already do naive selective prediction** (best 0.632 @0.2%). Meta-labeling is the principled upgrade,
  but it is **bounded by the same ~0.51 primary edge** we proved is near coin-flip. It can only help if
  "primary is right" is *conditionally* predictable. Our own findings (autocorr −0.03, 97% USD-factor)
  suggest the conditioning surface is thin — hence **test the conditional-accuracy buckets first**.
- Our prior **economic-calendar / event-timing** work was redundant with time-of-day; meta-labeling
  *conditioning* on those same features is unlikely to add much for the same reason.
- **Fractional differentiation is the one genuinely untried orthogonal feature source here** and is the
  highest-upside item, because every prior feature set was return-based (d=1).

**Ranked:**

- **HIGH — Conditional-accuracy bucketing pre-test (item #2).** ~1 hour; falsifies or motivates the whole
  vector. Do this before anything else.
- **HIGH — Purged K-fold + embargo + CPCV harness, and DSR/PBO deflation.** Even if meta-labeling fails,
  this *retroactively validates or kills* our existing 15m and 3-second results. Highest methodological ROI;
  we may currently be over-trusting point estimates.
- **HIGH — Fractional-differentiation features** on price level / spread / basket residual. New orthogonal
  signal; cheap; the most likely source of *actual* lift.
- **MED — Full meta-labeling layer (triple-barrier meta-labels + M2 filter)** on the best primary. Worth
  building *iff* the HIGH pre-test shows ≥1 bucket with accuracy materially >0.55. Otherwise it only
  reshapes coverage.
- **MED — Sample uniqueness + sequential bootstrap** for the meta-model (only meaningful once meta-labeling
  is built; de-biases overlapping 15m events).
- **MED — Conformal selective head** as a guaranteed-coverage alternative to `|p−0.5|` thresholding; small
  experiment, clean abstain semantics.
- **LOW — Focal loss / cost-sensitive M2.** Only relevant after reframing to rare high-edge setups; tuning
  knob, not a signal source.
- **LOW — Calendar meta-conditioning.** Redundant with our prior time-of-day finding.

**Bottom line for this vector:** meta-labeling is the *correct framework* for our selective goal but is
**not a source of new edge** — it cannot turn a 0.51 primary into 0.75 unless the "primary-correct" event
is itself predictable, which our mechanistic findings make doubtful at 15m. The genuinely additive items
here are **(a) fractional-differentiation features** (new orthogonal signal) and **(b) the AFML validation
stack (purged/CPCV + DSR/PBO)**, which protects us from declaring victory on a statistical fluke.
